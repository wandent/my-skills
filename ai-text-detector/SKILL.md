# AI Text Detector

Analyze text to determine if it was likely generated by AI using evidence-based linguistic criteria.

## Overview

This skill applies linguistic research to detect characteristics commonly found in AI-generated text. Rather than a binary detection system, it evaluates **how likely** the text is AI-generated across multiple dimensions, acknowledges areas of uncertainty, and explains the reasoning.

The analysis focuses on **observable linguistic patterns** rather than trying to identify a "watermark" or single telltale sign—because different AI models produce different outputs, and human writing varies widely.

## Analysis Framework

The skill examines text across six linguistic dimensions:

1. **Style & Tone** — Is the voice impersonal or overly formal?
2. **Vocabulary & Diversity** — Is word choice repetitive or generic?
3. **Repetition & Patterns** — Are structures, phrases, or ideas reused?
4. **Grammar & Structure** — Is the text too consistently well-formed?
5. **Expression & Naturalness** — Is emotional or sensory language present?
6. **Overall Impression** — Does it prioritize clarity over originality?

## Analysis Strategy

- **Scan for strongest signals first** — Some indicators (like highly consistent sentence structure or absence of emotion/idiom) are more reliable than others
- **Weight the evidence** — A few strong signals outweigh many weak ones
- **Account for context** — Technical writing, academic papers, and formal documentation have legitimate reasons to sound formal or precise
- **Acknowledge limitations** — Human writers can be very consistent, and some AI outputs are very human-like
- **Be honest about uncertainty** — If signals are mixed or context is ambiguous, say so

## Key Distinctions

**AI-generated text often has:**
- Consistent grammatical perfection with low sentence-length variation
- Generic phrasing and abstract language
- Lack of personal voice, idioms, or genuine emotion
- Repeated structural patterns (parallel sentences, lists, predictable flow)
- Absence of specific sensory details or anecdotes
- Neutral tone regardless of subject matter
- Text that prioritizes clarity and completeness over personality

**Human-written text often has:**
- Variation in sentence structure and length
- Specific examples, personal voice, or subjective perspective
- Idioms, wordplay, or colloquialisms
- Emotional expression (even in formal writing)
- Unique phrasing and vocabulary choices
- Natural mistakes or less-formal constructions

## What This Skill Cannot Do

- Detect watermarks or forensic AI fingerprints (it uses linguistic patterns, not technical analysis)
- Identify *which* AI model generated text
- Distinguish between different forms of machine text (LLM vs. other algorithms)
- Guarantee 100% accuracy (human writing can sound very "AI-like," and sophisticated AI can sound very human)
- Replace tools like GPTZero or Turnitin that use different technical mechanisms

## When Confidence Will Be Low

- Text written by domain experts (highly polished, consistent)
- Highly technical or specialized writing (restricted vocabulary for a reason)
- Very short passages (too little data)
- Translated text (may have unusual patterns)
- Text deliberately written to imitate AI or human styles
- Edited text (human + AI collaboration makes attribution hard)

## Getting the Most From This Skill

**Provide good context:**
- Tell me who likely wrote it or under what circumstances
- Share whether you have reasons to suspect AI involvement
- Note if the text is translated or edited

**Be specific about what you're looking for:**
- Trying to catch student cheating?
- Verifying content authenticity?
- Checking your own writing for "AI-ness"?
- Different goals may emphasize different signals

**Interpret the results thoughtfully:**
- A "High" likelihood is a flag to investigate further, not proof
- A "Low" likelihood doesn't guarantee human authorship
- Confidence levels reflect the clarity of evidence, not certainty
